Why it goes unanswered

The alternatives each fail differently

Option one
Bought contact lists

Strangers who never asked for anything. Low reply rates, and the sender carries the spam risk.

Option two
Advertising platforms

You pay to interrupt people who were doing something else. Cost per lead rises as intent falls.

Option three
Reading comments by hand

It works. It is also six hours a day that no founder or salesperson actually has.

Only the third one answers a person who already asked. AIZU makes the third one survivable.

How it works

Five steps, and only one is yours

01
Brief

You describe what you sell and who you serve. Once, in plain language.

02
Attach

The engine joins a browser session you already opened — or an official API.

03
Read

Each post is scored for relevance, then every comment beneath it.

04
Qualify

Commenters who signal intent become leads, with the fields you asked for.

05
Triage

Six platforms land in one inbox — tagged, staged, ready to answer.

Step 01 needs a person. Steps 02 to 05 repeat on a schedule, without one.

Defensibility

The safety rules are the product

Session
Attach, never launch

It joins a browser the operator already opened and logged into. It never opens one, never asks for a password, never stores one.

Pacing
Daytime only

Sessions halt outside roughly 8am to 9pm and pace with randomised pauses. No overnight bursts, no three-in-the-morning spikes.

Cost
Capped spend

Every model call is budgeted against a limit set on the campaign. On breach it degrades and raises a flag rather than overspending.

Access
Roles on the server

Four roles, checked server-side on every request. The interface mirrors them; it is never the thing that enforces them.

A challenge or CAPTCHA is never solved automatically. The run stops and waits for a person.

Local relevance

Built here, and shaped by here

  • 01Tashkent time is in the core. Scheduling runs on UTC+5, not as a user setting.
  • 02Local-first by design. One file, one machine — no dependency on stable bandwidth.

Honest standing

What is proven, and what is not

Proven — inspect it today
  • Six platform engines run end to end.
  • 1,661 automated tests pass on every change.
  • Billing, roles and multi-tenancy are wired and working.
  • Panel, worker fleet and desktop app are all usable now.
Not yet — no claim made
  • No paying customer. No revenue. None.
  • Terms of service and privacy policy are unwritten.
  • The Uzbek audio tier has never run against live traffic.
  • Throughput beyond a handful of parallel runs is untested.

We would rather be believed than impressive. Both columns are on this slide for that reason.

Risk

Three real risks, stated first

RiskHow it is handled today
Platform terms and account blocks Read-only on five of six platforms; daytime pacing; challenges are never solved automatically; the write subsystem is off by default. Structural, not removable — managed.
Personal data of people who did not opt in Only public posts are read, never private messages. A retention, consent and deletion policy is the first item on the roadmap, and the reason we are applying.
Model cost volatility A hard spend cap per campaign, enforced per call. A local-model path already exists and removes the external dependency entirely if needed.

None of these are hypothetical. All three have code behind the mitigation, not a plan.

Source: docs/architecture/engines.md §9; docs/architecture/overview.md §6.15
Roadmap

Twelve months, four milestones

QUARTER 1
Compliance base

Terms of service, privacy policy, retention and deletion. Written before the first customer, not after the first complaint.

QUARTER 2
First paying accounts

Fifty self-serve accounts. Verify the Uzbek audio tier against live traffic or drop the claim entirely.

QUARTER 3
Concurrency

The distributed worker fleet becomes the default path, removing the one-run-at-a-time ceiling. Two further platforms.

QUARTER 4
Agency channel

Multi-client agency tier and a partner route to market. First export revenue formally reported.

Compliance first, customers second. That ordering is deliberate, and it is where the programme helps most.

Sequencing commitment, not a revenue forecast.16
